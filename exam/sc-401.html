About the course

What SC-401 actually covers

Microsoft Purview, in the era where Copilot can read everything a user can. Three evenly weighted blocks: classifying and labelling information, stopping it leaving and deciding how long it is kept, and then the detection side — insider risk, alerts and activity. The official course title now says 'in the AI era', which is an honest description of why this exam has got busier.

Assumed knowledge: Microsoft 365 administration experience and a working grasp of compliance concepts.

The problems it answers

Why people in this role end up sitting SC-401

  1. Sensitive data nobody can locate, in a tenant that has been accumulating since 2016.
  2. A labelling scheme designed by committee that users bypass within a week.
  3. DLP policies in audit mode for two years because nobody dares turn enforcement on.
  4. Retention obligations that conflict — legal wants it kept, privacy wants it gone.
  5. Copilot turning a decade of quiet oversharing into answers somebody can read today.

Hands-on labs

14 hands-on labs

Fourteen lab exercises including setup. Compliance roles first, then sensitive information types and sensitivity labels, and on into data loss prevention, retention and the risk and alerting work.

For training partners

Selling SC-401

Everything a partner needs to put this course in front of the right customer. Lift any of it straight into a proposal.

“Copilot has made their oversharing visible. This is the course that gives somebody the tools to fix it.”

When to bring it up

  • A Copilot rollout paused or slowed over what it might surface.
  • An audit, NIS2 or ISO review asking where sensitive data actually lives.
  • DLP sitting in audit mode indefinitely because the false positives were never tuned.
  • A near miss — data shared externally that should not have been.
  • They bought E5 or a compliance add-on and use a fraction of Purview.

Who signs: Compliance lead, data protection officer, or the CISO. Legal sometimes has a view and occasionally the budget.
Who attends: Microsoft 365 and compliance administrators, and the security people who have inherited Purview.

What to sell next

  • SC-500 for the Azure and AI workload security around it
  • SC-300 when the findings turn out to be access problems
  • AB-650 for the Copilot administration side
  • A data discovery exercise on their own tenant, which often funds the training

Pushbacks you will hear

And a straight answer for each

We have DLP already.

Most organisations have DLP in audit mode. The gap is usually tuning and enforcement, which is a third of this exam.

Purview is enormous — can four days cover it?

It covers the three areas the exam weights evenly. eDiscovery and the deeper records management are a separate conversation, and worth saying so.

Our data is a mess, training will not fix that.

True. The course gives them the classification and labelling approach to work through it — and the honest answer is that it is a programme, not a week.

Can the security team not pick this up?

They can, but it is usually a different person. Purview rewards someone who will own it continuously rather than visit it after an incident.
