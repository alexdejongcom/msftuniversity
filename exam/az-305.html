About the course

What AZ-305 actually covers

The Azure architect exam, and the step up from operating to designing. Infrastructure design carries the largest share, followed by identity, governance and monitoring, then data storage and business continuity. It is case-study heavy by design: the questions give you requirements and constraints and ask what you would build, which is a different skill from knowing what the services do.

Assumed knowledge: AZ-104 level Azure operations experience. This exam assumes you have run the platform before designing on it.

The problems it answers

Why people in this role end up sitting AZ-305

  1. Designs that work technically and fail on cost, or the reverse.
  2. Business continuity promised in a contract and never actually tested.
  3. Identity and governance bolted on after the architecture was agreed.
  4. Data storage chosen for familiarity rather than access pattern, then regretted at scale.
  5. An architect who can describe services but cannot defend a design under questioning.

For training partners

Selling AZ-305

Everything a partner needs to put this course in front of the right customer. Lift any of it straight into a proposal.

“They have people who can build anything in Azure and nobody who decides what to build. That is the gap this fills.”

When to bring it up

  • Cloud spend rising without a corresponding design authority.
  • A migration programme about to start with no target architecture.
  • Their best AZ-104 people are ready for the next step.
  • A failed or painful disaster recovery test.
  • A customer or regulator asking for an architecture document they do not have.

Who signs: Head of architecture, CTO, or the programme funding a migration.
Who attends: Azure administrators and engineers moving into architecture, and architects new to Azure.

What to sell next

  • AZ-104 first for anyone without the operations grounding
  • SC-100 for the security architecture counterpart
  • AZ-700 and AZ-400 to deepen specific parts of the design
  • An architecture review engagement, which often follows naturally

Pushbacks you will hear

And a straight answer for each

Our architects already design on Azure.

Then this is validation and vocabulary, which matters if they are presenting designs to customers or auditors. If not, spend the money elsewhere.

It is the same material as AZ-104.

The services overlap, the skill does not. AZ-104 asks how to configure it; AZ-305 asks whether you should.

Case studies feel academic.

They are the closest thing to the actual job. Most architecture failures are requirement failures, not technical ones.

We use a lot of third-party tooling.

Fine — the design principles carry. Just set expectations that the reference architectures are Microsoft's.
